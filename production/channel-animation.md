# Channel animation direction and reusable production workflow

The channel covers everyday psychology and task avoidance. The user wants clothed stick figures **acting through a story**, including future long-form videos. A rendered slideshow, static presenter with an occasional moving wrist, or a successful encoder/rig test does not satisfy that preference.

For title, thumbnail and opening alignment, also read [channel-packaging.md](channel-packaging.md).

## Direction before rendering

For every scene, plan from the actual narration and aligned word times:

1. State what the character wants, what obstructs it, and the visible outcome.
2. Give the action an anticipation, a decisive movement/contact, a result and recovery. Do not replace these phases with a pan, zoom, subtitle change or unrelated nod.
3. Let gaze/head, shoulder/hip support and relevant hands participate. Keep supporting feet/seat/objects grounded. Small motion can be appropriate, but it must communicate the current thought/action.
4. Bind every held object to the actual owner hand. Show pickup, support, placement and withdrawal; never make a cloth/plate vanish as a shortcut.
5. Keep meaningful hand/prop contacts and both arm silhouettes legible in the encoded phone-size image. Technical visible-alpha tests do not catch every same-color silhouette problem.
6. Use a contextual action view, a necessary detail and a reaction where the meaning calls for them. Avoid repeatedly showing the same desk, icon, underline or advice card.
7. Change front/three-quarter/side assets only at authored view cuts unless genuine in-between art exists. A bitmap replacement is not a continuous turn.

## Production loop for Shorts and long videos

- Reuse approved script, original-rate voice, word alignment and identity. Do not regenerate speech or initiate a paid call without the corresponding authorization.
- Choose the hardest 5–10-second body/contact sequence first. Compare it against the prior iteration at the same narration time. Check actual intended-speed motion and sound, not only contact sheets or a count of changing pixels.
- Use the installed B6 review/reference requirements. Geometry, file hashes, stance stability and full decode are technical evidence; keep creative/source comparison and actual user approval separate. Never fabricate a review or infer approval of unseen output.
- Once the direction is accepted, extend with the same standard through the middle and ending. A request for a routine correction does not require asking the user to repeat an existing authorization.
- For long-form work, divide the actual script into narrative scenes and maintain a shot/action ledger. Add the missing actions/sets needed by the story; do not stretch a Short or loop this small action library for minutes. Track prop state, character knowledge, emotion and destination across cuts.
- Review every scene and the complete encoded film. Preserve narration order/rate, semantic single-line captions, native-format composition, balanced original/licensed audio, grounded contact, arm contrast and actual story progress. Keep unavailable playback/listening and unreviewed capabilities explicit.
- Deliver MP4, sidecars, source/checkpoint, rights and concise QA. No YouTube publication unless separately requested.
