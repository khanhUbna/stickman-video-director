# Psychology Facts — creator voice

## The viewer

An English-speaking adult who recognizes a small, frustrating behavior in daily life: avoiding an important email, reaching for a phone, replaying a conversation, or feeling guilty about a break. Speak to one person with curiosity and warmth. Do not assume every viewer is American, employed, living alone, clinically anxious, or free to ignore responsibilities.

## Spoken language

Natural American English. Use contractions, familiar verbs, concrete objects and short varied sentences. The creator reviews an English script followed by its complete Vietnamese translation. Humor comes from recognizable behavior and consequences, not from shaming the viewer or diagnosing them.

## Story and rhythm

550–650 spoken words, approximately 4–5 minutes when natural delivery and purposeful physical beats are measured together. Start with an observable moment and a curiosity gap. Keep a single central question, escalating choices, an earned psychological insight and a visible changed choice at the ending. Use the seven-part framework in profile.json.

Every passage should give the actor a desire, an obstacle, an action or a reaction. Prefer varied domestic/work/public settings when they serve the story. Avoid repeating one seated/standing pose under new words. A quieter ending may hold a reaction after the payoff; constant arbitrary motion is not the goal.

## What sounds wrong

No generic channel introduction, early subscribe request, fake shocking statistics, unsupported brain/chemical claims, long lists of life advice, interchangeable motivational endings, or padded repetitions. Avoid inflated phrases such as “unlock your potential,” “game changer,” “delve into,” “rewire your brain instantly,” and “psychology proves.” A hook must promise something the actual story delivers.

## What we can claim

Read and cite credible scientific evidence. Say what was studied, what remains uncertain and whether a mechanism is a possible explanation. A relatable example is fictional illustration, not a published experiment. Do not turn ordinary behavior into proof of trauma, ADHD, addiction or another diagnosis. Do not invent a paper, number, quote or research consensus. If a source cannot be read, record it as unverified and avoid attributing claims to it.

## Packaging

One emotional conflict, one dominant readable image and concise cover text. Title, thumbnail and opening contribute complementary information. Score tools are editorial lint, not view forecasts. An approved four-word thumbnail is not automatically invalid because an upstream rule of thumb prefers three.

## Production preferences

Keep the established gray-haired green-waistcoat painted character unless the creator changes the channel identity. The [faster 15-second movement trial](https://github.com/khanhUbna/stickman-video-director/tree/a315040/deliveries/rest-motion-trial-v1) was preferred to the earlier slow stand/sit treatment; that delivery also preserves the source/identity package on the video-delivery branch. New acting is authored at physical beat timings, with supported feet, hand/prop ownership and an actual result. Do not imitate faster acting by globally speeding up accepted narration.

## Costs and approvals

Default to text/research/storyboard/metadata and existing authorized media only. No paid API calls and no YouTube publication. Script review comes before full production; existing explicit approvals persist. No global Claude configuration or API key is needed for these installed tools.
