## Sources actually inspected

1. Sirois, F. M., & Pychyl, T. A. (2013). Procrastination and the Priority of Short-Term Mood Regulation: Consequences for Future Self. Social and Personality Psychology Compass, 7(2), 115–127. DOI: 10.1111/spc3.12011. Institutional record: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/
   - Inspected accepted manuscript abstract, introduction and discussion of task aversiveness, present/future self and short-term mood repair (manuscript pp. 3–6 and following discussion).
   - Film wording: “One influential account…” and “can offer relief right now” reflect an explanatory account, not a universal cause or a claim that caring necessarily produces procrastination.
   - Bills/interest and folder-as-verdict are original explanatory metaphors, not measured brain mechanisms or data charts.
2. Sirois, F. M. (2014). Procrastination and Stress: Exploring the Role of Self-compassion. Self and Identity, 13(2), 128–145. DOI: 10.1080/15298868.2013.763404. Institutional record: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91791/
   - Inspected abstract and discussion, including manuscript pp. 18–20. Associations across four samples and the cross-sectional limitation are explicit in the manuscript.
   - Film does not claim self-compassion cures procrastination or that the observational results establish causality. The practical contrast is curiosity about a lapse versus self-insult, without declaring the deadline optional.

## Other material and limits

- The originally proposed Gollwitzer/Sheeran meta-analysis was not obtained; its specific research-benefit claim was removed before synthesis. Implementation intention is introduced as a cue–action planning concept and a practical experiment, with no quoted effect size or guaranteed result. Do not represent that meta-analysis as newly read.
- Steel (2007) was an initial bibliography lead; it is not presented as a directly inspected primary source here. The verified Sirois/Pychyl review discusses related task-aversion literature. No unsupported numerical claim is narrated.
- Maya, her job/portfolio and her eventual application are fictional illustrations, not testimonials or study participants. The film explicitly does not invent a job offer.
- The 10-minute work window, three rough sentences, quiet breakfast window and next-action note are practical experiments, not scientifically validated thresholds or treatments.
- Strategic waiting, workload and lack of resources are distinguished from emotional avoidance. The professional-support caveat avoids diagnosis.
