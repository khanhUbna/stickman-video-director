# Editorial review — V2

This is an editorial judgment, not measured viewer retention, a view forecast or a promise of performance.

## What changed from V1

- A single evening now has a cause-and-effect progression: optional email → expanding task → expired break timer → friend's question → recognition → attempted rest → returning trigger → visible changed response.
- The friend now changes the scene. The protagonist's unsent message is the midpoint event, rather than a narrator merely stating a double standard.
- The closing door/sink interaction completes the opening. The protagonist does briefly repeat the old behavior before noticing it, making the decision less effortless.
- Three repeated chores were removed. The laptop, phone, timer, snack, remote, lock, drying rack and faucet give different actions with results.
- The roommate sees the protagonist sit down. This supports the final line's literal action.
- The clean plate comes from a drying rack, separate from the snack dish. Prop continuity must preserve that distinction.

## What to protect when directing

- Show the unsent “haven't earned it yet” clearly and briefly. Do not replace it with a generic motivational slogan.
- Do not repeat the entire cold open at the ending; match the trigger and advance beyond it.
- Paragraphs 8–10 are the highest pacing risk because they include the scientific interpretation and necessary scope. Keep reaction shots and a single imaginative receipt gag; no repeated standing presenter.
- The external-pressure paragraph should be delivered plainly and briefly. It prevents blame without becoming a separate lesson.
- The ending allows discomfort to remain. Do not add a transformation montage or claim that one evening cured a psychological problem.

## Local tool result and deliberate exception

The current default scriptcheck report is saved under checks/scriptcheck.json. It checks word count, punctuation, paragraph lengths, sentence lengths and opening language using fixed editorial rules. Its opening-score gate is the only expected error; retain the actual result rather than lowering the threshold or claiming all checks passed. The opening's direct title question, audible trigger, fictional cue and clean-plate mystery are retained on editorial grounds. The keyword scorer does not establish their retention quality. Five hook alternatives and their actual scores are in hooks/ and checks/hooks.json.

No paid service or audience analytics were used. Scientific verification is recorded separately in research/factcheck.md.
