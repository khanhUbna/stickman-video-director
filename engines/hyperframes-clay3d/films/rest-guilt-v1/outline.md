# Why You Feel Guilty When You Rest — V2

Central question: Can the protagonist let another person see them resting without defending it?

1. **Hook — paragraph 1:** A clean plate is being washed when someone comes home. The opening asks why resting feels like getting caught. Explicit fictional frame: “Picture this.”
2. **Open Loop / Setup — paragraphs 2–3:** Flash back one hour. Urgent responsibilities are handled; an optional email interrupts a deliberately chosen movie break. The viewer knows the clean-plate moment is coming but not whether the character will hide.
3. **Build-up — paragraphs 4–5:** One reply produces more work. The break timer expires while the movie has never started. This is an observable consequence rather than another list of chores.
4. **Midpoint Turn — paragraphs 6–7:** A friend receives permission to rest. Their innocent question about the movie makes the protagonist type, then reject, “haven't earned it yet.” The character sees their own rule through a concrete interaction.
5. **Psychological Insight — paragraphs 8–10:** Conditional interpretation about self-worth; limited actual leisure/enjoyment association; acknowledge practical external pressures. No diagnosis, invented research or universal cause.
6. **Climax — paragraphs 11–13:** The protagonist closes the laptop, starts the movie, then hears the keys. The old impulse briefly wins. Seeing the clean plate interrupts it; the character turns off the faucet and answers honestly.
7. **Payoff / Emotional Ending — paragraphs 14–15:** The roommate sees the protagonist sit down; the feared inspection never happens in this invented scene. Guilt can remain while the choice changes. Final payoff is being seen resting without disguising it.

No additional advice list, subscribe request or second ending. The dialogue and roommate's reaction are invented, not quotations or study observations.
