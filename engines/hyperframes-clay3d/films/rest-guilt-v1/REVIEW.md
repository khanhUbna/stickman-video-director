# Review — full clay film

## Creative decision

Follow the approved one-evening story, preserving the gray-haired green-waistcoat identity in 3D clay. The clean plate returns as an earned payoff. Separate domestic actions, device details, an imagined receipt inspection, a short external-pressure montage and a quiet ending replace a static lecturer. No alternate storyline or new narration was substituted.

## Fresh visual pass

A reviewer who did not author this animation read STORYBOARD.md and all three 63-shot sheets. It identified phone thumbs hiding the pivotal unsent draft, an ambiguous key silhouette, the written reminder being lost behind the laptop, and a repetitive horizontal-arm reaction. These were corrected by lowering the hands to the phone bezel, exposing a toothed brass key and lock, raising the paper onto the desktop with its own overhead insert, and changing the reaction to an interrupted reach and withdrawal. A copy of the pre-fix source was kept in the ignored cache. Actual encoded motion and audio were not assessed by that reviewer.

## Measurements and actual inspection

The browser QA audits every authored pose for reachable fixed-length limbs, foot contact, caption geometry and deterministic seeking. It also records one actual browser frame for every shot and blocks external requests. The preflight separately checks HyperFrames static/runtime/layout/contrast. Its expensive craft/motion heuristic is explicitly disabled; our full pose audit, caption measurements, shot-frame review and encoded hard-action clip cover different concerns and do not constitute that heuristic pass.

The local synthetic voice is retained at native recording speed; no external TTS or paid API. Original music/Foley are reused and synchronized. Loudness and true peak are measured, not heard. No human audition or real-time listener approval is claimed.

## Deliberate limits

The native clay pose cadence is 12Hz, with 30fps delivery repeating frames. Faces convey expression and gaze; phoneme lip sync is not implemented. The imagined receipt close-up is the most literal illustrative shot; it is clearly introduced as an imaginary scenario. The ending deliberately settles into rest. The completed-film technical results belong in the delivery QA report; source checks alone are not evidence that an MP4 passed.
