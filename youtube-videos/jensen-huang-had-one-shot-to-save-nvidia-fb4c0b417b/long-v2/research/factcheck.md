## Truth boundaries and omission check

The central story is supported as Huang’s later account. S2 independently corroborates functioning, highly rated RIVA hardware with limitations in 1997. It does **not** corroborate the original first tape-out, exact resource runway, ICOS closure, or internal leadership conversation. The script correctly calls the review a later revision and limits its evidentiary use. The six-month opening is approximate recalled runway, not a documented deadline.

No unsupported private feelings, fictional investor refusal, board confrontation, or claimed scientific psychology mechanism was found. Audience invitations (“imagine,” “think,” “you might expect”) are narrative framing. The script does not claim that the company skipped testing, that no silicon screening happened later, that there could never be later revisions, or that a single production batch was all NVIDIA could manufacture. The commitment concerns the initial design cycle under resource constraints.

The emulation equipment remains correctly identified. Paragraph 19’s broader use of “simulation” matches Huang’s words at S1:57 and does not redefine the emulator as a software-only simulator. The eight-of-32 blending figure is interview testimony, introduced by the host and acknowledged by Huang; it is not independent API documentation. The script neither generalizes that number to the whole chip nor claims a fully hardware transform/projection pipeline.

The script includes market conditions, game developers, uncertain outcomes, and the later-revision caveat. No omission requires another factual correction. The lines “Customers could find value” and “It kept a route forward open” stay at the level of a defensible possibility/survival inference rather than an unsupported quantified sales or causal assertion.
