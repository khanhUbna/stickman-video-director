# Research brief: the RIVA 128 one-attempt decision

Scope: a single narrator tells the 1997 RIVA 128 crisis, using explicitly labeled counterfactuals to explore the cost of waiting. Creator's current channel is animated historical/business storytelling, overriding the repository's default everyday-psychology niche. 550–650 spoken words remains a useful pilot length. No paid media or publication.

Saved complete extracted text as sources/acquired-huang-2023.txt, with original speaker labels and sourcekit line numbering. The actual episode audio has not been independently listened to. ## Events and permitted inferences

1. The hosts place the crisis in 1997; Huang himself confirms that time at line 28. NVIDIA's earlier architecture was incompatible with DirectX and RIVA 128 reset the company (24–29).
2. The ordinary physical-chip/software/debug/re-spin sequence would outlast their resources, according to Huang (36–42). Six months and no affordable/time-feasible redesign/re-tape-out are his retrospective account, not audited financial figures.
3. He called ICOS, which he says was shutting that day for lack of customers, and bought its emulation equipment (36). Keep the attribution. Do not invent location, dialogue, tears, a shutdown deadline to the minute, or travel.
4. Emulation let the team run its software/QA before the real chip was manufactured. They waited in the lab for Windows output (43–44, 56–57). Do not say they skipped testing or built a virtual reality demo.
5. The team went directly to production on the taped-out design (45–54). This was not a blind untested bet.
6. The hosts mention eight of thirty-two DirectX blend modes; Huang acknowledges that they should have implemented all thirty-two (20–23). This is one category of functions; never depict the entire chip as 75% broken. No fabricated missing-features customer confrontation.
7. Retrospectively he emphasizes bringing future risk forward through emulation/testing, rather than urging indiscriminate bets (55–59). He also describes multiple decisions/team work, not one lone genius saving a company in isolation (27–29, 231–235).
8. Whether another chip firm would have taken NVIDIA's place, or the same decisions would work elsewhere, cannot be established. Alternate outcomes are rhetorical possibilities, never historical findings.
