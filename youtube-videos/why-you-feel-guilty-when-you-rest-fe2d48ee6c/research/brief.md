S1: Jeff Grabmeier, Ohio State News, 23 August 2021. The complete institutional article was read. Local source lines 38–42 describe an association between beliefs that leisure is wasteful and lower enjoyment. The article links the journal record, but the publisher page remains blocked and its full paper was not read.

S2: Crocker and Wolfe, Contingencies of self-worth, Psychological Review (2001), PMID 11488379. The PubMed bibliographic record and complete abstract were read. The verified DOI is 10.1037/0033-295X.108.3.593. The abstract describes a theoretical model in which self-evaluation responds to successes/failures in personally important domains. It does not establish a chores-to-guilt causal pathway or identify a viewer's childhood experiences.

S3: Masicampo and Baumeister, Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology (2011), PMID 21688924. The PubMed record and complete abstract were read; verified DOI 10.1037/a0024192. The abstract reports experimental cognitive activation/interference effects and their reduction after specific plans. It does not establish that plans eliminate guilt or anxiety.

Exact URLs, short quotations and limitations are in sources.json and source-excerpts.md. Full cached pages/abstract records remain in the private research working directory, not the public Git delivery. No full-paper or systematic-review reading is claimed.

## Application to this script

Paragraph six says research has linked seeing leisure as wasted time with enjoying it less. S1 supports this association. The next sentence explicitly says this does not explain every case of guilt. No depression/anxiety causation, prevalence or effect-size statistic is inferred.

The usefulness-as-price interpretation in paragraph six is introduced with maybe. S2 supports the general model, while this household example remains fictional and qualified. Paragraph four describes this fictional character's response to chores rather than asserting a clinical avoidance mechanism.

Paragraph eleven offers a concrete reminder and return time as an optional experiment. S3 provides limited background, not a promise of emotional or therapeutic benefit. Paragraph thirteen explicitly allows the guilty thought to remain. Paragraphs nine and ten distinguish real caregiving, deadlines and unfair workloads from discretionary chores.
