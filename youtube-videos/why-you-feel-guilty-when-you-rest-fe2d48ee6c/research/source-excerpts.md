## S3 — Specific plans and unfinished goals, abstract

E. J. Masicampo and Roy F. Baumeister. **Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals.** Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2011. PMID: 21688924. DOI verified in the retrieved PubMed record: 10.1037/a0024192.

Record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/

Actual retrieved record: https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=21688924&retmode=xml

Evidence, abstract (local extracted source line 9):

> “Allowing participants to formulate specific plans for their unfulfilled goals eliminated the various activation and interference effects.”

The abstract discusses cognitive activation/interference in experimental tasks; it also separates those effects from changes in goal-related emotions. This does not establish that writing a reminder removes guilt, treats anxiety or works for every person. The script offers a concrete reminder as a small optional experiment without promising those outcomes. Full paper not retrieved/read.
