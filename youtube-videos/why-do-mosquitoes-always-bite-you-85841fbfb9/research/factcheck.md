## Review decisions and limits

- **Skin chemistry:** supported as shorthand for compounds found on skin, with both bodily and microbial contributions. Paragraph8 correctly keeps the acid finding associative and rejects a universal single ingredient. It does not justify removing acids, using cleaner, or depicting the identified low-volatility acids as a proven long-distance scent trail.
- **Cue order:** “First” and “as she approaches” stage this fictional encounter. Paragraph4 explicitly says there is no neat checklist. Retain that limitation; the available passages do not establish a fixed CO2 → heat → odor algorithm or distance thresholds.
- **Saliva and bumps:** CDC directly supports saliva causing the reaction and differences in reaction/appearance time. “Not a perfect bite counter” and “isn't necessarily an untouched friend” are reasonable qualified inferences; neither asserts that the friend was bitten or that asymptomatic bites occur at a known rate.
- **Ending:** the offended mosquito is a gag. It is not evidence that repellent or clothing always stops feeding. Keep label-following advice and avoid strengthening the ending or metadata into guaranteed protection.

The writer adopted the review's minor precision suggestion in paragraph2: **“clues you may not notice”** replaces the earlier absolute wording. The final wording and hash above were rechecked. No other narration change was requested or made by the reviewer.

Paragraph1's five guests, burger and three bumps, the chair experiment, friend dialogue and mosquito personality are invented staging, not study counts or observed cases. The “sweet blood” line is explicitly the fictional friend's theory; the narration does not endorse a blood-type ranking. Attraction alone is not a diagnosis of hygiene or a medical condition. No misleading scientific omission was found for the narrow question addressed.
